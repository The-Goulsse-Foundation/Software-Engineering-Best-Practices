PODCAST 02 — NAMING VARIABLES, FUNCTIONS, AND MODULES
Approximate runtime: 5 minutes

One of the highest-leverage software engineering skills is naming.

JavaScript code is read far more often than it is written. A good name lets the reader understand intent without opening another file.

Prefer names that communicate meaning. Instead of data, consider userProfile, invoiceItems, or searchResults. Instead of process, describe the action: calculateTax, validateEmail, or publishEvent.

Boolean variables deserve special attention. Names such as isActive, hasPermission, canRetry, and shouldCache tell the reader that the value represents a condition.

Functions should generally describe what they do. A function called saveUser communicates more than a function called handleData.

Avoid misleading abbreviations. Shorter is not automatically clearer. A few characters saved today can create confusion months later.

Be careful with generic words such as manager, helper, utility, and service. They can be appropriate, but they often hide responsibility. A function called formatCurrency tells you more than a function called helper.

Naming also creates an architecture. If your project consistently distinguishes repositories, services, controllers, validators, and domain objects, the vocabulary helps engineers understand how pieces interact.

Do not optimize names for brevity at the expense of comprehension. But do not create enormous names that encode an entire paragraph either.

A practical rule is that names should answer the question: what is this thing responsible for?

Good naming is a form of documentation that stays next to the code.
