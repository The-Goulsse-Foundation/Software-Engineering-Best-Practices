PODCAST 63 — SCALABILITY FUNDAMENTALS
Approximate runtime: 5 minutes

Scalability means handling increased workload without unacceptable degradation. Start by identifying what grows: requests, users, records, files, connections, or computation. Then identify the bottleneck. CPU-bound work may require more compute or parallelism. I/O-bound work may require better concurrency or fewer round trips. Database bottlenecks may require indexing, query changes, caching, partitioning, or different data modeling. Horizontal scaling means adding instances. But state stored locally can make horizontal scaling difficult. Stateless request handling often simplifies scaling. Queues can separate producers from workers and absorb bursts. Rate limits protect shared resources. Backpressure prevents memory from growing without bounds. Do not confuse scalability with speed. A system can have low latency at small scale but fail as workload grows. Load testing should use realistic traffic patterns and data volumes. Scaling decisions should be driven by measurements rather than assumptions. The best optimization is often removing unnecessary work before adding infrastructure.
